The Cellula Institute: First Week

A microscopy escape-room day. Students arrive as new research assistants, discover the institute's cell lines have been mixed up, prove what each sample is from evidence, then join a research lab to test how those cells respond to stress.

Part 1 · about 90 minutes including induction

Cell authentication

Five unlabelled samples (A–E) and six suspects on the list: MEFs, Sf9 insect cells, Paramecium, E. coli, baker's yeast, and fixed kidney cells as a decoy. Groups move freely between six benches in any order, collecting evidence into their logbook. When every sample has a claim backed by evidence, they talk it through with an education officer, who decides if they're ready.

Part 2 · about 50 minutes

Join a research lab

During the break, the institute's freezer defrosts. Prof. Castellan explains that some cells warmed up and others were already part of planned stress tests (drugs, heat, pH). Each group of six chooses one of five labs, each tied to a cell they identified in Part 1 and to a real scientist's research. They design the experiment on an iPad, run a real plate on the FLUOstar, gather supporting images, and build a one-slide pitch.

Study design links

  • Apoptosis as regulated cell death
  • Disrupted cell cycle and cancer cells
  • The plasma membrane: integrity, PI uptake, dye uptake
  • Key science skills: questions, variables, controls, data analysis

Practical design

Staff stress the cells before the day, so the plate is real but fits into 50 minutes. Students add the readout dye, run the FLUOstar and import the results into their logbook.

Close · about 15 minutes per lab

Lab meeting

Each of the six groups in the room gives a two-minute pitch from their one slide: the question, what they did, one graph or image, and what it means. The coaching scientist responds as a colleague. Prof. Castellan closes with what each real lab has actually found.

How it's built

The digital pieces

Everything runs in the browser on GTAC iPads, bench computers and the big screen. One logbook record per group connects every station, stored on the GTAC server.
