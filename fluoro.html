Activity summary

Story: the life of a CELL

Unicellular organisms include parasites and aquatic organisms. Each one has adapted to a single-celled lifestyle, so it has to carry out MRS GREN (Movement, Respiration, Sensitivity, Growth, Reproduction, Excretion, Nutrition) without help from tissues or a colony. At each microscope, students look for evidence of MRS GREN and the features that show how a structure helps with "the life of a cell". In the workbook they tap each process they can see evidence of, then justify it.

Students follow one single-celled organism, Paramecium, across three microscopes. They watch it swim live on the stereo microscope, look at a methylene blue stained slide on the compound microscope, then stain live cells with MitoTracker Red to see the mitochondrial network on the EVOS fluorescence microscope.

Samples

  • Live Paramecium on the stereo microscope, on one side of the bench
  • Methylene blue stained Paramecium: a prepared slide on the compound microscope
  • Live Paramecium in a 12-well plate, one plate shared per bench side, for MitoTracker staining

Three microscopes, three views

  • Stereo: whole live cells, swimming and changing direction
  • Compound: methylene blue shows the macronucleus and micronucleus, plus the oral groove and vacuoles
  • EVOS: MitoTracker Red (RFP channel) shows the mitochondrial network. Transmitted light shows cilia, vacuoles and the cell membrane.

Why the 12-well plate

  • MitoTracker Red works only in living cells, and it eventually kills them
  • Each stained well can only be used once. Having 4 wells to stain gives several groups multiple chances to stain and image.
  • 4 unstained live control wells stay healthy all session for comparison

Activity summary

Students prepare and image fixed cells on an EVOS M5000 fluorescence microscope to find the nucleus, cytoskeleton and mitochondria for their scavenger hunt. This station uses two cell types: SF21 insect cells and mouse embryonic fibroblasts (MEFs). This station uses one cell type, mouse embryonic fibroblasts (MEFs), in two conditions: healthy and diseased. Students compare the two.

Samples

  • SF21: insect (moth ovary) cells, fixed, grown directly on the base of the well
  • MEFs: mouse embryonic fibroblasts, fixed, on round coverslips
  • Healthy MEFs: mouse embryonic fibroblasts, fixed, on round coverslips
  • Diseased MEFs: the same cells under the disease condition, fixed, on round coverslips. Disease model: TBC

How samples are presented

  • One 6-well plate per bench side. 4 wells are used. Column 3 is left empty.
  • The lid is labelled with the sample name and colour stickers showing which stain each well gets
  • SF21 and MEF wells use the same addition method, so one protocol covers both
  • Column 1 is healthy and column 2 is diseased. Both get exactly the same stains, so the condition is the only difference.
  • The DAPI/FITC wells (row A) are MitoTracker Red pre-stained before fixing, shown by a dashed red ring on the lid

Imaging

  • The whole plate goes into the EVOS M5000 caddy, so no slides are needed
  • Fluorescence channels: DAPI (blue), GFP (green, for FITC) and RFP (red, for MitoTracker)
  • Methylene blue wells are imaged in transmitted light. They can also be explored on the fluoro scope if students are interested.
  • Images are uploaded to the iPad workbook

How the plate gives multiple chances to stain

  1. Row A has 4 wells of live Paramecium for staining. Row B is left empty. Row C has 4 live control wells.
  2. A group takes the next row A well that isn't struck through.
  3. Before adding anything, they strike through that well on the lid with the black Sharpie and write their initials and the time.
  4. They add 20 µL MitoTracker Red and 20 µL Protoslo. After a few minutes the mitochondria glow red.
  5. MitoTracker eventually kills the cells, so each stained well has a short imaging window. The time on the lid shows the next group how long that well has had stain in it.
  6. If a group misses the window or wants another go, they take the next well. There are 4 chances per plate.
  7. The row C control in the same column is never stained. Students compare it with their stained well for movement, shape and cell features.
